# The Global Conversation Crisis: A Research Brief for CAMMUPLABS

**Bottom line:** A convergent body of evidence from the U.S. Surgeon General, the WHO, the OECD, Gallup, Pew Research Center, Cigna, peer-reviewed journals, and government statistics offices in the UK, Japan, and Korea now establishes that the world is in an objectively measurable "epidemic of loneliness and disconnection," driven by a collapse in both the *quantity and quality of meaningful conversation* — across generations, in workplaces, in classrooms, and at the family dinner table. This brief consolidates the strongest globally resonant data points CAMMUPLABS should use to replace Korea-only statistics on its website and to anchor investor pitches, paired with Korean datapoints where the Korean audience requires them.

## TL;DR

- **The world is officially in a connection emergency.** The U.S. Surgeon General (May 2023) and the WHO Commission on Social Connection (2023–2026) have declared loneliness a public-health priority on par with smoking; WHO estimates social disconnection contributes to **more than 871,000 deaths per year globally**, and roughly **1 in 6 people worldwide (15.8%) experience loneliness**, with adolescents 13–17 the loneliest cohort at **20.9%**.
- **The collapse is conversational, not just relational.** Americans aged 15–25 spend **45–50% less time socializing face-to-face than in 2003** (BLS American Time Use Survey, 2024). The share of U.S. adults with **no close friends has quadrupled since 1990**, from 3% to 12% (Survey Center on American Life, 2021). Only **30% of U.S. teens say smartphones help people their age learn good social skills** (Pew, 2024). MIT's Sherry Turkle calls it a "flight from conversation" producing "a crisis of empathy."
- **The economic and human costs are massive — and Korea is at the leading edge.** Workplace loneliness costs U.S. employers an estimated **$154 billion/year** (Cigna). **20% of the world's employees experience daily loneliness** (Gallup, *State of the Global Workplace 2024*). Lacking social connection raises premature-mortality risk by **26–32%** — equivalent to smoking 15 cigarettes a day (Holt-Lunstad meta-analyses; HHS, 2023). Korea ranks near the bottom of the OECD on social support: only **61% of Korean men 70+** have someone to count on, versus an OECD average of **89%** (OECD *Inclusive and Sustainable Well-being in Korea*, 2025).

## Key Findings

### 1. Generational Disconnect — Families, Workplaces, and the Gulf Between Generations

#### Parent–child conversation breakdown

- **U.S. — Pew Research Center, "How Teens and Parents Approach Screen Time" (March 11, 2024; n = 1,453 teens and their parents):** **46% of U.S. teens say their parent is at least sometimes distracted by their phone when the teen is trying to talk to them**; about **four-in-ten parents and teens regularly argue about phone time**. Only **30% of teens say smartphones help people their age learn good social skills**, and roughly **40% say smartphones make it easier to develop healthy friendships**.
- **U.S. — Gallup / Walton Family Foundation, "Parents Avoid Hard — but Helpful — Conversations With Gen Z" (2024; n = 1,675 parent–teen pairs):** Only about **half of parents say it is easy to discuss their child's feelings or social-media life**, even though **83–92% of children say those very conversations are helpful**. Asked what they want from parents when upset, **62% of children said they just want their parents to listen**; only 28% wanted advice — a textbook signal of unmet conversational need.
- **UK — Baumann et al., *Journal of Marriage and Family* (2019), peer-reviewed analysis of the UK Time Use Survey (2000–2015):** "Alone-together time" — when children and parents share a location but do not interact — rose **43%** over 15 years, and mobile devices were used during **47% of alone-together time** by 2015. The data quantify the lived reality of "together but not talking."
- **Korea — ChildFund Korea (초록우산어린이재단), *아동행복생활시간 보고서* (2017 baseline, updated 2024):** Korean children and adolescents spend an average of just **13 minutes per day with family (0.9% of their day)** versus **190 minutes on out-of-school study** and **84 minutes on TV/smartphones/media**. Only **53.7% of Korean parents have daily conversations with their children, versus an OECD average of ~70%**.
- **Korea — Statistics Korea (통계청) Time Use Survey:** Time Korean students spend with family fell from **59 minutes/day in 2009 to 29 minutes/day in 2014** — a more-than-50% decline in five years; the longest-running official Korean data point on family conversational time.
- **Korea — PMI 가족조사 (May 2025):** **13.7% of Koreans in their 20s say they "never have conversations with family"** — 2.7× the rate among Koreans in their 60s (5.0%).

#### Generational gaps in the workplace

- **Global — Gallup, *State of the Global Workplace 2024* (n = 128,000+ employees, 160 countries):** **20% of the world's employees experience daily loneliness.** Among **employees under 35 the rate is 22%**, versus **19% among those 35+**. By location: **fully remote workers 25%, hybrid 21%, fully on-site 16%.** Engaged employees are **64% less likely** to feel lonely than disengaged employees. Gallup's headline: *"Twenty Percent Of The World's Employees Experience Loneliness While Global Employee Engagement Stagnated And Employee Wellbeing Declined."*
- **U.S. — Cigna *2020 U.S. Loneliness & the Workplace Report* (n = 10,441 adults):** **61% of Americans were lonely (up 7 points YoY), including 69% of employed Millennials and over 80% of employed Gen Zers.** Lonely workers were **2× more likely to miss work for illness, 5× more likely to miss work due to stress**, and **2× more likely to intend to quit within 12 months**.
- **U.S. — Indeed multigenerational workforce survey (1,000 job-seekers + 1,000 employers, 2024):** "Different communication styles and workplace expectations among generations" was named the **top workplace problem** by **40% of job-seekers and 42% of employers**. (Note: the related $62 billion-per-year figure widely cited in HR press traces to David Grossman / SHRM's "Cost of Poor Communications" study, which actually estimated an average loss of **$62.4 million per large company with 100,000+ employees** — important to cite correctly.)
- **U.S. — Salesforce, *Global Digital Skills Index* (2022; n = 23,000 workers across 19 countries):** *"Only 32% of Gen Z feels very equipped with the resources to learn the digital skills needed to thrive"* in the workplace — a baseline number for the under-prepared generation entering the workforce.
- **U.S. — LivePerson global communications survey (n ≈ 4,000 18–34-year-olds across the U.S., UK, Australia, France, and Germany, 2017):** **~74% of Millennials globally communicate digitally more often than they do in person** — the most defensible cross-national figure for the channel mismatch with older generations.
- **U.S. — BLS / SHRM (2024):** The U.S. workforce is now **15% Boomer / 31% Gen X / 36% Millennial / 18% Gen Z** — five generations working side by side, with the widest communication-style gulf in modern labor history.

#### Time on devices vs. time talking

- **U.S. — BLS *American Time Use Survey*, 2003–2024:** Adults' time spent face-to-face socializing fell **~30%** over 20 years; for teenagers it fell **~45–50%**. Fifteen- to twenty-five-year-olds now spend **39 minutes/day socializing in person, down from 61 in 2003** — about 158 fewer hours/year per young person.
- **U.S. — Pew Research Center, *Teens, Social Media and Technology 2024* and *Screen Time 2024*:** **95% of U.S. teens have a smartphone; nearly half are online "almost constantly."** Gallup estimated U.S. teens spent an average of **4.8 hours/day on social media** in 2023.

### 2. Lack of Conversational Depth — Talking More, Connecting Less

#### Empirical link between conversation depth and wellbeing

- **Mehl et al., *Psychological Science* (2010, n = 79 college students; replicated 2018 with n = 486 across four populations including breast-cancer survivors, divorced adults and meditators):** Using the Electronically Activated Recorder (EAR), researchers found the happiest people spent **~25% less time alone and ~70% more time talking to others**, and had **twice as many substantive conversations and one-third as much small talk** as the unhappiest people. Across all participants, 17.9% of conversations were small talk and 35.5% were substantive. Mehl: *"The happy life is social, rather than solitary, and meaningfully so."*
- **Sherry Turkle, MIT, *Reclaiming Conversation: The Power of Talk in a Digital Age* (Penguin Press, 2015; five years of ethnographic research across homes, schools, and workplaces):** *"We are being silenced by our technologies — in a way, 'cured of talking.' These silences — often in the presence of our children — have led to a crisis of empathy that has diminished us at home, at work, and in public life."* Turkle documents the *"rule of three"* — in groups of four or more, teens now accept that some members can be on phones so long as three are still verbally engaged — a candid measure of how social norms have eroded around full conversational presence.
- **U.S. Surgeon General Vivek Murthy, on *The Oprah Podcast* (Jan 2025):** *"We've moved from having confidants to contacts, from having friends to having followers — a shift from quality of friends to quantity. There are certain things you can just say to people online that you would never say to them in person."*

#### The shift from face-to-face to digital

- **HHS, *Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation* (May 2023):** Across all age groups, in-person time with others has fallen for two decades; among **15–24-year-olds, in-person social interaction with friends fell ~70%** over roughly two decades.
- **U.S. Friendship Recession — Survey Center on American Life (May 2021 American Perspectives Survey, n = 2,019; comparable to 1990 Gallup):** Americans with **no close friends rose from 3% (1990) → 12% (2021)** — and 15% among men. Those with **10+ close friends fell from 33% → 13%**. The share of men with **6+ close friends collapsed from 55% → 27%** in 30 years.
- **Distracted parenting — Pew (2024):** **46% of teens say their parent is at least sometimes distracted by their phone** during conversation; **half of parents admit looking through their teen's phone** — surveillance substituting for dialogue.

#### Classroom participation and student engagement decline

- **OECD PISA 2022 (Korea country profile):** Korean 15-year-olds had one of the OECD's **lowest rates of asking questions when they don't understand math** — only **33.8% do so "more than half the time" at least, ranking 75th of 79 economies**. Korean student life satisfaction ranked **66th of 73**, and **16% of Korean students reported feeling lonely at school**.
- **U.S. — SERU Consortium / Inside Higher Ed (April 2025):** Student engagement in academic, civic, career, extracurricular and research activities at major U.S. research universities **remains below pre-pandemic 2019 levels** even three years after reopening; one tracked indicator (students assisting faculty with research) is down **17%**.
- **U.S. — National Center for Education Statistics (2021), corroborated by Generation Wellness (2024):** Only **47% of U.S. students reported feeling engaged in their learning** — a sharp decline from pre-pandemic levels.
- **STEM higher-ed — Callis-Duehl et al., *CBE—Life Sciences Education* (2021, n = 73 undergraduate STEM students):** During the COVID shift online, students "participated less frequently in class discussions" while meeting professors more often outside of class — a documented contraction in classroom voice.

### 3. Loneliness & Social Isolation — The Epidemic of Disconnection

#### Government and global health authorities

- **U.S. Surgeon General Vivek Murthy, *Our Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation* (HHS, May 2, 2023):** *"Lacking social connection can increase the risk for premature death to levels comparable to smoking 15 cigarettes a day."* Even before COVID-19, **~50% of U.S. adults reported measurable levels of loneliness**. The advisory documents a **29% increased risk of heart disease, 32% increased risk of stroke, and 50% increased risk of developing dementia** among older adults with poor social connection.
- **U.S. Medicare cost — Surgeon General's Advisory / AARP / NACo (2023):** Social isolation among older U.S. adults accounts for an estimated **$6.7 billion in additional Medicare spending annually**.
- **WHO Commission on Social Connection (established November 2023; flagship report *From Loneliness to Social Connection* released June 30, 2025; co-chaired by Dr. Vivek Murthy and African Union Youth Envoy Chido Mpemba):** Across 153 countries, **15.8% of people experience loneliness**, including **20.9% of adolescents 13–17** and **24.3% of people in low-income countries**. The Commission estimates **over 871,000 deaths per year worldwide are linked to social disconnection** (more than 100 per hour). In May 2025 the **World Health Assembly adopted the first-ever resolution on social connection (WHA78.9)**. Murthy: *"In this Report, we pull back the curtain on loneliness and isolation as a defining challenge of our time."*
- **OECD, *Social Connections and Loneliness in OECD Countries* (2024) and *How's Life? 2024*:** Across 25 OECD countries, **3–11% of people report feeling lonely "most or all of the time"**; **10% of OECD residents have no one to rely on in times of need**; and **across 22 European OECD countries, 8% of people have no close friends**.
- **OECD, *Inclusive and Sustainable Well-being in Korea* (2025):** Only **61% of Korean men and 65% of Korean women aged 70+** report having someone to count on in time of need, versus **OECD averages of 89% and 87%** — among the lowest social-support scores in the developed world. *"Social isolation is increasing concern in Korea, in part driven by a rising share of single-person households."*

#### Country-level loneliness ministries

- **United Kingdom — first in the world:** Appointed a Minister for Loneliness in January 2018 (Tracey Crouch initially; Stephanie Peacock as of 2024). The originating **Jo Cox Commission on Loneliness final report (December 10, 2017)** — building on British Red Cross / Co-op research — found that **more than 9 million people in Britain (≈14% of the population) often or always feel lonely**. ONS chronic-loneliness rate among adults 16+ moved from **6% (2018) to 7% (December 2023)**, with **28% of UK adults feeling lonely "often, always, or some of the time"**; an estimated **3.83 million Britons are chronically lonely** (ONS, 2023), up from 3.24 million in the first year of the pandemic. The UK has invested over £80 million across the Building Connections Fund and Know Your Neighbourhood Fund.
- **Japan — second in the world:** Appointed a Minister for Loneliness and Isolation in February 2021. The Cabinet Office's *Survey on Awareness and Lifestyles of Children and Young People* (released March 31, 2023; n = 13,769) found that **2% of Japanese aged 15–64 — approximately 1.46 million people — meet the criteria for *hikikomori*** (severe social withdrawal lasting 6+ months). Japan's *Act on the Promotion of Measures to Address Loneliness and Isolation* took effect **April 1, 2024**, establishing a Cabinet-level Headquarters for Promotion of Measures for Loneliness and Isolation chaired by the Prime Minister.
- **Korea — Ministry of Health and Welfare, *2024 고독사 실태조사* (released November 27, 2025):** **Solitary deaths (고독사) rose to 3,924 in 2024**, up from 3,661 in 2023 (+7.2%) and 3,279 in 2020 (+20% over 5 years). Single-person households reached **36.1% of all Korean households in 2024**. Per Statistics Korea's 2023 사회조사 (Social Survey), the national "social isolation rate" (사회적 고립도) stands at **33% — one in three Koreans aged 19+** have no one to turn to either for help with housework or for a needed conversation.
- **Korea — Korea Institute for Health and Social Affairs (한국보건사회연구원 / KIHASA, May 2023; endorsed in Ministry of Health and Welfare's first-ever *2023 고립·은둔 청년 실태조사*, December 13, 2023):** Approximately **540,000 Koreans aged 19–34 (5.0% of the cohort)** are classified as 고립·은둔 청년 — isolated/reclusive youth — up from **3.1% (≈334,000) in 2019**. Among them, **~75% have considered suicide and ~50% report physical or mental-health problems.** An additional **~140,000 Korean adolescents aged 13–18** fit the category.

#### Cigna Loneliness Index — longest-running U.S. workplace dataset

- **Cigna 2018 U.S. Loneliness Index (n = 20,000):** UCLA Loneliness Scale score of **44; 46% felt left out sometimes or always**.
- **Cigna *2020 U.S. Loneliness & the Workplace Report* (n = 10,441):** **61% of Americans lonely** (up 7 points YoY); **73% of very heavy social-media users scored as lonely** versus 52% of light users — a 20+ point gap that widened across the late 2010s.
- **Cigna / Morning Consult, *Loneliness Epidemic Persists* (December 2021):** **58% of U.S. adults considered lonely**.
- **Cigna economic-cost research (cited by Marketplace, HR Executive, 2023–24):** Loneliness costs U.S. employers an estimated **$154 billion annually** through stress-related absenteeism (lonely workers miss **5+ additional workdays/year**) and elevated turnover (**2× intent to quit within 12 months**). Some downstream analyses sum total economic cost to **$300–406 billion** including productivity and healthcare. The **UK New Economics Foundation** estimated UK employer loneliness costs at **£2.5 billion/year**, of which £2.1 billion falls on the private sector.

#### Peer-reviewed evidence on mortality and health

- **Holt-Lunstad, Smith & Layton, *PLOS Medicine* (2010), 148 studies, n = 308,849:** People with strong social bonds are **~50% less likely to die over a given follow-up period** than those with weaker connections — an effect size comparable to smoking cessation.
- **Holt-Lunstad et al., *Perspectives on Psychological Science* (2015), 70 studies, n = 3.4 million:** Social isolation increased mortality risk **29%**, loneliness **26%**, and living alone **32%**; results held across gender, follow-up length and world region, but were stronger for samples with average age under 65.
- **Holt-Lunstad in *American Psychologist* / APA member feature (2024):** *"There have indeed been dramatic shifts in how people are connecting and interacting socially now. But the majority of the epidemiological data we have that established this as a risk factor for premature mortality was conducted before widespread use of most of these technologies."* — i.e., the underlying disconnection trend predates smartphones, which amplify but do not solely cause it.

#### Robert Putnam — *Bowling Alone* (1995/2000; Revised and Updated, Simon & Schuster, 2020)

Putnam's revised 2020 edition continues to document a long secular decline in U.S. social capital across the post-1965 period: collapses in church attendance, union membership, PTA participation, civic/fraternal organizations, neighborly trust, and informal socializing. The 2020 update adds new evidence on the role of generational replacement, suburbanization, and the privatizing influence of screen-based entertainment — themes Murthy and the WHO Commission now operationalize as health policy.

#### Sherry Turkle — *Reclaiming Conversation* (Penguin, 2015) and ongoing MIT research

Turkle's five-year ethnographic study across homes, schools and workplaces concludes that face-to-face conversation *"lays the groundwork for empathy"* and is the single best predictor of psychological development in adolescents. The central diagnosis: *"We are too busy connecting to have the conversations that count."* Her parallel finding — that **people now find solitude so uncomfortable they reach for their phones to escape even brief silence** — underwrites the public-health framing that disconnected dialogue is not an absence of activity but an over-saturation of low-quality input.

## Recommendations

For CAMMUPLABS' "Problem Definition" website section and investor deck, prioritize the highest-credibility, most globally portable data points in this order:

1. **Lead with mortality and scale.** Open with the WHO + Surgeon General "15 cigarettes a day" framing (Holt-Lunstad 2010/2015 → Murthy 2023 → WHO Commission 2025). This is the single most-cited, most-resonant data point in the field and is now triple-anchored by government-level bodies. It legitimizes the entire problem space for any investor.

2. **Pair every Korean statistic with an OECD comparator.** Don't say "Korea has a 고독사 problem" — say "Korea has one of the OECD's lowest social-support scores: only 61% of Korean men 70+ have someone to count on, versus 89% in the OECD average" (OECD, *Inclusive and Sustainable Well-being in Korea*, 2025). This converts a Korea-specific problem into a "Korea is the leading indicator" thesis.

3. **Use generational data to differentiate workplace value.** Gallup's 22% (under-35) vs. 19% (35+) daily-loneliness split, Cigna's 80%+ Gen Z loneliness, and the 25% (remote) / 21% (hybrid) / 16% (on-site) split are the three cleanest workplace data points for any B2B SaaS or enterprise-wellness positioning. Salesforce's 32% Gen Z digital-skills figure and Indeed's 40–42% top-problem finding round out the multigenerational pitch.

4. **Use Mehl and Turkle to defend the product thesis.** The Mehl studies are the empirical core of the "depth, not just quantity" argument that CAMMUPLABS' product presumably operationalizes. Turkle is the most quotable researcher in the entire space — her language ("flight from conversation," "crisis of empathy," "we are too busy connecting") is purpose-built for landing pages.

5. **Stage your evidence for fundraising:**
   - **Pre-seed / seed:** Lead with the Surgeon General + WHO declarations to legitimize the problem space.
   - **Series A:** Layer in Gallup workplace + Cigna $154B economic cost to prove enterprise willingness-to-pay.
   - **Series B+:** Add Korean ministry-level cohort data (KIHASA 540,000 isolated youth; MoHW 3,924 lonely deaths; Statistics Korea 33% social-isolation rate) + OECD bottom-quartile rankings to position Korea as both leading-indicator market and public-policy tailwind.

6. **Benchmarks that would update these recommendations:** If the Gallup global daily-loneliness rate falls back below 15%, or if U.S. teen face-to-face socializing rebounds above its 2003 baseline, or if WHO downgrades the 871,000-deaths estimate in its 2026 follow-up, the problem narrative weakens. Re-check Gallup *State of the Global Workplace*, the BLS American Time Use Survey, the WHO Commission, and Korea's annual MoHW 고독사 실태조사 each year.

## Caveats

- **The Mehl "substantive conversation" finding is correlational, not causal** — happier people may simply have deeper conversations, not the other way around. The 2018 replication explicitly found small talk is not actively harmful; it serves as a "social inactive ingredient" enabling depth. Frame accordingly: CAMMUPLABS sells better conversations; it does not claim conversation itself is a happiness pill.
- **Eric Klinenberg (NYU sociologist) is a credentialed dissenter** who has written *"There is no good evidence that Americans are lonelier than ever"* — arguing the data show declining face-to-face socializing without necessarily proving rising subjective loneliness. Acknowledge this in long-form materials; the ATUS time-use decline data are rock-solid even where the "epidemic" framing is contested.
- **Cigna's $154B/year U.S. employer-cost figure** is widely cited but is an internal Cigna calculation, not peer-reviewed. The downstream $300B and $406B figures rely on Cigna's underlying math; cite Cigna directly and avoid the larger numbers in formal materials.
- **The "$62 billion per year" cost of poor communication** that circulates in HR press is misattributed. The original Grossman / SHRM figure is **$62.4 million per large company** with 100,000+ employees, not $62 billion across all U.S. companies. Do not use the $62 billion figure.
- **The "72% of Millennials and Gen Z prefer text vs. 18% of Boomers" datapoint** circulating in marketing blogs as "Pew Research" does not appear in any verifiable Pew report. The defensible analogous figure is **LivePerson's 2017 global survey** showing ~74% of Millennials communicate digitally more than in person. Cite LivePerson, not Pew.
- **Hikikomori counts (1.46M in Japan, 2023)** rely on Cabinet Office self-report surveys with varying year-to-year criteria; the Cabinet Office itself cautions against direct cross-year comparison.
- **Korea's "540,000 isolated youth"** is a KIHASA-modelled estimate applying a 5.0% prevalence to the 2021 19–34 population, not a direct census. The MoHW December 2023 survey corroborates the order of magnitude.
- **Sample sizes vary.** Pew's 2024 teen study: n = 1,453. Cigna 2020: n = 10,441. Gallup *State of the Global Workplace 2024*: 128,000+ employees across 160 countries — the strongest comparable dataset globally. Use Gallup when an international audience needs a single headline figure.
- **Korean parent–child conversation data sources are heterogeneous** — ChildFund Korea (NGO, time-diary), Statistics Korea (government), and commercial polls (Visang Education, PMI, Hyungji Elite). Treat the Statistics Korea Time Use Survey (29 min/day in 2014) as the most authoritative figure; treat NGO numbers as illustrative.
- **Country-level chronic-loneliness rates (e.g., UK ONS 6–7%) are lower than U.S. Cigna numbers (~58–61%)** because of measurement differences (UCLA Scale cut-off vs. single-item "often or always" question). Match the metric to the audience and never imply the U.S. and UK figures are directly comparable.
